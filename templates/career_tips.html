A few useful notes

Small ways to make your story clearer.

Practical reminders for making your resume more useful to the person reading it.

01 · WRITING

Lead with what changed

Instead of listing responsibilities alone, explain what improved because you did the work. Use a real number when you have one; never guess to make a bullet sound bigger.

Try: “Improved [process] by [what changed], helping [who] to [outcome].”

02 · TAILORING

Make the match easy to see

Read the job description and identify the experience it emphasizes. Bring your relevant examples forward, using the employer’s terminology only when it accurately describes your work.

Before applying, check that your top section supports this specific role.

03 · READABILITY

Give the reader room

Consistent headings, clear dates, and enough white space help a recruiter find what matters. A simple layout is often easier for both people and software to read.

Print preview is a quick way to check page breaks and spacing.

04 · ACCURACY

Keep it honest

Every statement should be something you can comfortably explain in an interview. AI suggestions are a starting point; verify every detail before using it.

Specific and true will always beat impressive but vague.
